# Diagnosis

## Contents
- Finding format

## Finding format
Read first, then report. Start with the revision and the scope actually read (“已读 scene_1–scene_4 全文；scene_5 只读了索引”). Never call unread scenes checked. Each finding has:
- **Where**: scene or block ids and a short quote (two lines at most).
- **Type**: checkable (broken reference, untracked object, a contradiction you can quote) or craft (interpretive).
- **Evidence and confidence**: what supports it, and what would make it wrong.
- **Proposal and effect**: a concrete change and what it does to the scene. For craft findings, state the outcome to reach, not a rewritten scene.

A later edit can invalidate a finding: re-read the passage before applying a fix.

## Knowledge and continuity
- Keep three orders apart: when something happens in the story, when the text shows it, and when each character and the audience learn it. The audience does not know a flashback before it is shown.
- A line proves only that it was said. 甲说“我从没去过钟楼”，后来闪回里甲在钟楼藏信: report a statement that differs from a shown event — perhaps a lie or a planted reveal, not automatically an error. Quote the actual speech act; “我不知道信在哪” is not a question.
- Shown action counts as event unless the text marks it unreliable. Do not invent amnesia, false flashbacks or secret knowledge to explain a contradiction away.
- Objects: track location, possession, pickup and handover. 伞留在长椅边，下一场却在手里: point to the missing pickup; do not claim it happened off-screen. A dent, scar or stain needs an origin and a shot that can show it.
- Time and space: travel time, day and night, who can see whom. Flag assumptions that matter for filming (车里坐得下三个人吗？窗外看得到站台吗？).

## Craft findings and false positives
Report a pattern only with at least two locations and a stated loss:
- causal repetition — answers arrive through strangers, coincidence or the opponent confessing;
- tactic repetition — everyone accuses, denies, raises their voice;
- over-explaining — the action or a line already shows the choice, and the next line translates it;
- over-closing — every scene ends on a summary or a punchline, so the next one must restart the pressure;
- even density — every beat carries the same amount of news, so nothing lands as an event;
- costs settled at once — every cost is repaid inside the episode, so nothing accumulates.

Not formulaic by itself: a ritual repeated so that its second time inverts the meaning; a running gag where each repetition costs someone new; a locked two-shot used as pressure; an accepted series format; a character repeating an action because the story is about being stuck. When unsure, write a note or a question, not a fix.

For a silent or object-driven short, judge visible action, sound, blocking, pauses and exchanges. Two anonymous people and one place are a valid film. Offer staging options with their trade-offs instead of explanatory prose.

## Severity and verdict
- blocker: the script cannot be followed or handed off (broken structure; a contradiction that changes the plot);
- major: clearly damages understanding, continuity or the intended effect;
- minor: a concrete cost that does not block;
- note: a taste choice or optional polish.

End with a verdict — 可以继续 / 建议修改 / 信息不足 — and group proposals by scene. Apply nothing until the person chooses what to change; then save only that scope.
